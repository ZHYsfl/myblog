AI-Whys now has an AI Agents volume! It has become the third major macro module, after DL (including the audio-dl extension) and RL.

AI Agents are the current darling of the AI world. Many people assume they are just about gluing APIs together and writing prompts, but this is actually a field with extremely high engineering barriers. When building RL Infra, for example, different models often require their own parsers, and training and inference must remain strictly consistent. Without a thorough understanding of agentic RL and the underlying logic of AI Agents, it is impossible to know where to begin.

Based on this insight, AI-Whys has organized the underlying theory of agents (Figure 1). The latest macro panorama (Figure 2; see the URLs at the end for the high-resolution image and its source) shows that AI Agents essentially grow out of online RL and Transformers.

Knowledge from books always feels shallow. AI-Whys understands that theory alone is not enough, so it has also open-sourced Experiment 42: lightweight AI Agent frameworks in both Go and Python (with a TypeScript version to come). They support streaming inference, parallel tool calls, watchdog-based time-limited inference, and three memory mechanisms.

By the way, VoxFlow—my cascaded, full-duplex, multimodal, real-time interactive agent system (technical report linked at the end)—is built on a plus version of this Go framework that I built myself.
