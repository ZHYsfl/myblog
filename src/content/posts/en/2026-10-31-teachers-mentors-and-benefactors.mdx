Today, I want to talk about the teachers, senior mentors, and benefactors who have had a profound influence on me.

## From Mathematics to Computer Science

In July 2023, after finishing the gaokao, I happened to meet Professor Liu Junming online. He is a teacher at the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences and runs a forum community. The forum was still in its early days, and membership cost less than 2,000 RMB (it seems considerably more expensive now). I consulted him before placing Jilin University ahead of some of my other college choices.

At the time, he held group meetings on Java. I attended several and found myself deeply interested in programming. I had originally planned to major in mathematics—a decision I had made in my final year of high school, before the gaokao. But two things changed my mind. First, my mother consulted a college admissions advisor, who said that someone who liked mathematics but not physics could still study computer science and earn a good living. Second, I was genuinely fascinated by programming, which felt to me like the same kind of logical reasoning found in mathematics. So, apart from mathematics at Shandong University, I chose computer science for every remaining preference.

I was eventually admitted to Jilin University for computer science. Because I had studied Java over the summer, learning C in my first semester was not particularly difficult. For my path into computer science, I am grateful to my mother, the admissions advisor, and Professor Liu Junming for their guidance and inspiration. Looking back today, I truly like—and love—what I study in computer science.

## Becoming More Socially Aware and Finding the Goal of Entrepreneurship

In late February 2025, I was recovering from heartbreak, and I began to grow from a completely idealistic boy into someone more socially aware. Inspired by the Bilibili creator Larry, I began to think of entrepreneurship as my ultimate goal in life.

In late April 2025, I was influenced by red-pill ideology, especially LeonG's videos at the time. Although some of those ideas seem extreme to me now, they played an almost decisive role while I was becoming more socially aware and recovering from heartbreak. I began to focus on building masculinity, working on myself, exercising, and similar things. I also became more determined to become extremely outgoing, to fight for the opportunities and resources I wanted, and to make entrepreneurship my ultimate goal in life.

## Professor Li Bojie: A Guide in Technology, Innovation, and Life Direction

In late July 2025, I attended an AI Agent training camp in Beijing organized by Professor Liu Junming. An approachable man of ordinary height walked in, wearing rectangular glasses and presenting slides made with Slidev about the inner mechanisms of Claude Code. He was Professor Li Bojie: a computer science PhD from the University of Science and Technology of China's Special Class for the Gifted Young and its joint program with MSRA, a recipient of the CCF Outstanding Doctoral Dissertation Award, a former Huawei “Genius Youth,” and Chief Scientist at Pine AI.

He then gave us six problems and asked each team to choose one and spend five days working on it. As my team's leader, I came up with an architecture and went to Professor Li for advice. His patience and enthusiasm exceeded anything I had imagined. From today's perspective, my architecture was clearly still immature, but he listened patiently to my entire thought process and offered careful guidance. At the time, I had only heard from Professor Liu that Professor Li was an entrepreneur whose company had raised an enormous amount of funding that year, and I admired him greatly.

I added Professor Li on WeChat at the event. Later, I noticed that his WeChat Moments often contained remarkable blog posts, opinions, insights, and achievements, and I discovered his personal website, [01.me](https://01.me). I read nearly all of his articles, which profoundly changed my understanding of CS/AI technology, scientific innovation, entrepreneurship, and much more. From then on, I began to imitate what Professor Li had done when he was younger: delve into technology, write blog posts to share what I learned, build influence, and share my work on WeChat Moments.

The Bilibili creator 月球大叔 also said, “If I could go back to university, I would do four things: contribute to open source, create output, learn English, and invest” (I do not remember the exact words, but that was the idea). That is how this website was born. As of October 31, 2026, I had made more than 1,900 GitHub commits in the past year, which also grew out of the inspiration I received here. Many of Professor Li's later blog posts have continued to influence me greatly. His open-source book on AI Agents has also helped me enormously in completing my knowledge system in this area.

The problem I chose at the camp was to let a user chat with AI while the AI controlled the computer screen to help accomplish tasks. This aligned exactly with the broad direction of the PhD project I later applied for in SJTU's speech AI group: a cascaded approach to full-duplex dialogue. I spent a year refining that system, and it is now relatively mature.

It is fair to say that Professor Li guided my undergraduate study of LLMs and AI Agents, helped me understand what AI Infra does, shaped my understanding of scientific innovation and entrepreneurship, helped me mature socially, and inspired me to come to SJTU to work on speech AI—full-duplex dialogue systems and end-to-end large-model approaches. He is a role model for me.

## Research Taste: The Most Valuable Criticism and Advice

Later, I worked as a research assistant for Professor Feng Erhu at SJTU's IPADS. Professor Feng has had a profound influence on me. On July 12, 2026, I reported my research progress to him, and he harshly criticized my work, calling it a toy or a course project. I had desperately wanted to do it well and even thought Professor Feng would be impressed; instead, I received severe criticism and burst into tears on the spot.

That criticism made me deeply aware of my problems. I had done research without surveying the field, did not know what others had done or what the state of the art was, and was merely indulging myself. My taste was also very poor: I never surveyed existing work, chose bad datasets, made ideas unnecessarily complicated, and let my thinking become too diffuse and vague instead of focused. From these perspectives, I was a qualified engineer, but not even a passable researcher. That criticism made me deeply aware of my own problems.

Later, I came across the famous “letter of discouragement” written by Professor Jiang Yanyan of Nanjing University's computer science department, and learned that many students knew him and his operating systems course. I emailed him in the hope of having a conversation, and he readily agreed. We spoke online for 90 minutes, during which I learned that he was going to start a company. He gave me invaluable advice about my future development. He made it clear that my current problem was a lack of taste—exactly the problem Professor Feng had pointed out—but that this was normal because I was only a third-year undergraduate. The purpose of a PhD, he said, was to solve this problem.

He told me that during a PhD, I needed to find a trade-off between meeting KPIs and cultivating taste by reading classic papers from different fields and running open-source code. He also told me that if I could join Professor Feng, I absolutely should, because we both knew just how strong IPADS was.

## A New PhD Chapter

During the later mutual-selection stage, although I had been recognized as an outstanding participant in SAI's summer camp, I ultimately did not join Professor Feng because of my ranking. With both professors aware of the situation, I applied through the formal process for a PhD with Professor Zhang Wangyou at SAI—the project using the cascaded full-duplex approach—and passed the interview.

Professor Zhang is now my advisor. He is exceptionally kind and treats me with respect, tolerance, and care. At essentially every stage, I take the initiative to report my progress to him, and he always helps me patiently.

## Long-Term Thinking and Team Responsibility

In 2026, I also went to Beijing for an offline forum event, where I was deeply influenced by Deng Kan, a CMU PhD in embodied AI. Seeing that he was no longer young yet still starting a company, and that he had been named one of China's top ten entrepreneurs using AI to drive economic development in 2018 (if I remember the year correctly), inspired me greatly.

I see a PhD as an exercise in long-term thinking. Compared with the length of the rest of one's life, the additional years beyond a master's degree are very short. Age is not the problem. As long as you are capable, you can start a company, earn money, and accomplish things at any age.

From him, I learned what abilities and responsibilities a team leader should have. A leader must be the person on the team who can withstand the most pressure, must step forward first and set an example, and must make sure that no one on the team falls behind. I also learned from him that people should present themselves boldly. As I gradually saw more examples of successful people, these lessons inspired me to write [“Tolerance and Preferences: There Is No Right or Wrong, and All Roads Lead to Rome”](https://zhouhaoyang.me/posts/zh/tolerance-and-preferences-there-is-no-right-or-wrong-and-all-roads-lead-to-rome).

## With Gratitude

As of October 31, 2026, this is the full picture. I want to express my sincere gratitude to every teacher, senior mentor, and benefactor who has inspired, helped, and supported me. I hope that one day I, too, can become someone who helps, inspires, and influences others.
